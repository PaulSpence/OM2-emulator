## What the project is

A monthly emulator of ACCESS-OM2 1° (`1deg_jra55_iaf_omip2_cycle6`). It predicts 6 state fields (column heat content,
column freshwater content, SST, SSS, u_surf, v_surf) from the two previous months plus 4 surface forcings (net heat
flux, freshwater flux, tau_x, tau_y). The working notebooks are `notebooks/Ocean_Emulator_UNet.ipynb`
(deterministic) and `notebooks/Ocean_Emulator_UNet_CRPS.ipynb` (ensemble). Code under `src/Experiment/` is copied
verbatim into the notebooks and flagged; the tests in `test/` run in GitHub CI, never locally.

Hard rules from the user:
- The coupler reproduces ACCESS-OM2's real flux physics (CICE bulk formulae on 3-hourly JRA55-do, MOM's SSS
  restoring). The only neural network is the ocean emulator. Both training stages run coupled; there are no
  uncoupled baselines.
- No opt-in flags or config switches for fixes: just fix the code.
- Notebooks are human-readable, with no hidden helpers.
- Edits go only into `src/` and the two notebooks above.
- Use a persistent Jupyter kernel and rerun only the changed cells. Never rerun whole notebooks to test.
- Commit only when asked.

## Training

Each notebook trains in two stages. Stage 1 (`runs/<name>/shared`) has 90 epochs with rollouts of up to 12
months. Stage 2 (`runs/<name>/deterministic`) has 30 epochs with 48 free months plus 12 scored months. Both stages
are coupled: every month's forcing comes from `PhysicalCoupler`, applied to the emulator's own previous state.
`GlobalBudgetConstraint` closes the ocean-total heat and freshwater budgets exactly with the coupled fluxes.

Evaluation covers:
- a 20-year control that repeats the 1990-91 year;
- a 2005-2018 hindcast;
- the "largest |z| first above 20" stability metric.

## The problem being chased

The coupled emulator drifts (SSS and freshwater content in the control), and it over-responds to fluxes.

The diagnostic is a per-cell regression of the one-step content change on the flux anomaly, 2005-2018, area-mean.
It measures the response to a restoring-sized freshwater nudge and to 10 W/m2 of heat:

| | FW->FWC | Q->OHC | Q->SST |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACCESS-OM2 | 29.5 kg/m2 | 1.8e6 J/m2 | 0.023 K |
| network, stage-1-only checkpoint | ~35 | | |
| network, end of stage 1 | 41 | | |
| network, end of stage 2 | 71-77 | ~2e7 | 0.068 |

- The over-response grows with training-rollout length.
- The freshwater excess sits in the subtropical gyres and the Gulf Stream. About half the heat excess is in the
  equatorial cold tongues, where ACCESS-OM2's SST falls as the heat flux rises.
- Ruled out:
  - **Memory.** ACCESS-OM2's k-month response falls with k.
  - **Trends via the flux.** FWC trends do not follow the freshwater flux.

## Root cause found on 2026-11-27, fixed in 573baa9 but NOT yet retrained

The coupler computed `flux = F_true(t) - B(true surface of t-1) + B(emulator surface of t-1)`. Its "rest" therefore
held `B(true_t) - B(true_{t-1})`, which is ACCESS-OM2's own SST/SSS change over the month being predicted. The
network could read the truth off its forcing.

Measured over 1995-2004:
- This leak is 65% of the std of the heat-flux anomaly and 27% of the freshwater one.
- Its correlation with the true dSST is -0.37, and with the true dSSS +0.35.
- Removing it raises the flux's correlation with the true content change, from 0.10 to 0.16 for heat and from
  0.16 to 0.20 for freshwater.

The fix: the rest is now `F_true(t) - B(true surface of t)`, unlagged. The emulator's B stays lagged, which acts as an
explicit step.

## Dead ends, so they are not retried

- **ColumnBudget** (65eb5b7, removed in 573baa9). It forced each column's flux anomaly into its content, leaving
  the network only lateral transport. Persistence alone gave |z| ~300 within one month, because ACCESS-OM2's
  lateral transport cancels about 93% of a local heat-flux anomaly. Both seeds went NaN.
- **SSS restoring.** It is in the coupler (81ce332), but it did not stop the drift on its own.
- **Growth constraint.** Removed in 65eb5b7. Without it, seed 42's control first exceeded |z| > 20 at year 9.1,
  against 15.2 with it; seed 43 was unchanged (5.2 vs 5.1). If instability returns after the leak fix, revisit
  this. The experiment branch is `exp/no-growth-constraint`.

## Nvidia vs AMD

The code was developed on H200s (141 GB). Setonix has MI250X GPUs: 64 GB per GCD, ROCm.
- **Memory.** The 72 GB of weather no longer fits on the GPU. Cell 13 then keeps it in host memory automatically
  (`WEATHER_DEVICE = cpu` when `gpu_bytes < weather_bytes + 40e9`) and copies each batch's months to the GPU. That
  is slower; also check the training memory itself on 64 GB.
- **CUDA calls.** `torch.cuda.*` works under ROCm (HIP). `_records_sum_compiled` uses `torch.compile` (Triton);
  check that it compiles on ROCm, or fall back to `_records_sum`.
- **Allocator.** Cell 2 sets `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF`; ROCm reads `PYTORCH_HIP_ALLOC_CONF`.
- **Job scripts.** `jobs/run_notebook.pbs` is PBS-only and calls `nvidia-smi`; Setonix needs a Slurm equivalent.
- **Precision.** Lightning runs `precision="16-mixed"` (fp16 AMP; the bulk formulae and interpolation force fp32 inside). Check fp16 on MI250X before comparing numbers with H200 runs.
